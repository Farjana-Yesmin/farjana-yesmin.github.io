Hei! My Journey to (Trying to) Learn Norwegian

Published: November 11, 2025    |    Categories: Language Learning, Personal Reflection, Research Life

As I prepare for the next chapter of my research career, which may soon take me to a new country, I’ve realized that understanding the local language is the first step to truly connecting with a place and its people. So, a few days ago, I opened Duolingo and began my Norwegian journey — with exactly zero prior experience and one determined owl reminding me daily: “Don’t break your streak!”

Step One: “Hei!” and Other Victories

So far, I’ve learned how to greet people — “Hei!” — and order essential life-sustaining items like “kaffe” (coffee) and “melk” (milk). My proudest moment? Realizing that “Kaffe, takk!” means “Coffee, please!” and that “melk og kaffe” doesn’t mean “milk or coffee” (it’s “milk and coffee”). Small wins matter.

Screenshot from my latest lesson: all green checks except one stubborn “milk and coffee” error. Progress!

The Quirky Charm of Learning Through an App

My days now start with a cheerful green owl and a gentle guilt trip if I skip a session. I’ve been introduced to characters like Dag, Lisa, and Marius, and learned lines like “Jeg er en mann” (I am a man) and “Du er en kvinne” (You are a woman). It’s equal parts educational and slightly absurd — but that’s what makes it fun.

Small Lessons Beyond Words

Learning a language reminds me how progress often looks quiet and invisible — a few more words understood, a mistake avoided, a sentence spoken with slightly more confidence. It’s humbling, in the best way possible. Every small success in Norwegian feels like proof that patience and repetition still work, even in a world of instant everything.

Looking Ahead

I don’t expect to become fluent from an app, but I do hope that saying “Tusen takk” (a thousand thanks) and “Ha det bra!” (goodbye) will show a little respect when I arrive. The journey of a thousand miles begins with a single “Hei!”.

Current Duolingo streak: 5 days 🌱
Favorite word so far: “Kaffe.” Naturally.
