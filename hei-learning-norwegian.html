Practical Phrases I Can Now Use

With my expanded vocabulary, I can form more complex and practical sentences:

  • Hvordan går det? (How is it going?)
  • Det går bra med meg (I'm doing fine)
  • Går det bra med deg? (Are you doing alright?)
  • Beklager (Sorry)
  • Unnskyld (Excuse me, sorry, pardon)
  • Tusen takk (Thank you very much)
  • God dag (Good day, good afternoon)
  • God helg (Have a nice weekend)
  • Var så snill (Please)
  • Bare hyggelig (My pleasure, you are welcome)

Reflections on the Learning Process

I'm noticing patterns in the language now. For example, adding "-en" to the end of a noun often makes it definite (like "brød" to "brødet" for "the bread"). This is similar to English's "the" but attached directly to the word.

Some words have been particularly interesting to learn. For instance, "faren" means both "the father" and "the danger" - a reminder that language often carries cultural nuances and histories within it.

Looking Ahead

My next goal is to start Section 2, where I'll learn to order in a restaurant more comprehensively. I'm excited to practice phrases like "Jeg vil gjerne ha..." (I would like to have...) in real restaurant scenarios.

I'm also planning to supplement my Duolingo practice with listening to Norwegian music and watching simple Norwegian videos to improve my comprehension.
