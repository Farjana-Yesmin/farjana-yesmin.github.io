Hei! My Journey to (Trying to) Learn Norwegian

Published: November 11, 2025
Updated: November 20, 2025
Categories: Language Learning, Personal Reflection, Travel

Anyone who's followed my writing knows I love exploring cultures beyond the surface level. After documenting everything from sunsets in Camel's Back Park to International Mother Language Day celebrations abroad, I've always believed that language is the first key to truly understanding a place. That curiosity has now led me north—to Norway.

So, a few days ago, I opened Duolingo and began my Norwegian journey — with exactly zero prior experience and one determined owl reminding me daily: "Don't break your streak!"

Step One: "Hei!" and Other Victories

So far, I've learned how to greet people — "Hei!" — and order essential life-sustaining items like "kaffe" (coffee) and "melk" (milk). My proudest moment? Realizing that "Kaffe, takk!" means "Coffee, please!" and that "melk og kaffe" doesn't mean "milk or coffee" (it's "milk and coffee"). Small wins matter.

Progress Update: Current Duolingo streak: 14 days strong! The green owl and I are becoming good friends. I've completed the first 5 units covering basics, phrases, food, family, and shopping.

The Quirky Charm of Learning Through an App

My days now start with a cheerful green owl and a gentle guilt trip if I skip a session. I've been introduced to characters like Dag, Lisa, and Marius, and learned lines like "Jeg er en mann" (I am a man) and "Du er en kvinne" (You are a woman). It's equal parts educational and slightly absurd — but that's what makes it fun.

Small Lessons Beyond Words

Learning a language reminds me how progress often looks quiet and invisible — a few more words understood, a mistake avoided, a sentence spoken with slightly more confidence. It's humbling, in the best way possible. Every small success in Norwegian feels like proof that patience and repetition still work, even in a world of instant everything.

Why Norwegian?

I've always been fascinated by Scandinavian culture—the concept of friluftsliv (open-air life), their design philosophy, and the way they balance modernity with nature. If I ever get to visit Norway as a traveler, I want to be able to read street signs, order food authentically, and maybe even understand a local story or two. There's something special about being able to say "takk" instead of "thank you" when you're truly trying to connect with a place.

Basic Sentences I Can Form

Putting it all together, here are some simple sentences I can now construct:

  • Jeg spiser brød (I eat bread)
  • Du drikker kaffe (You drink coffee)
  • Jeg er en kvinne (I am a woman)
  • Du er en mann (You are a man)
  • Min far er snill (My father is kind)
  • Broren min er veldig smart (My brother is very smart)
  • Jeg vil ha kaffe og melk (I want coffee and milk)
  • Er du sønnen eller broren? (Are you the son or the brother?)

Looking Ahead

I don't expect to become fluent from an app, but I do hope that saying "Tusen takk" (a thousand thanks) and "Ha det bra!" (goodbye) will help me connect more genuinely if I ever visit. The journey of a thousand miles begins with a single "Hei!".
