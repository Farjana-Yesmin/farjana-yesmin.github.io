Learning from Mistakes – Reviewing & Reinforcing

I have been diligently working through my mistake reviews. Some of the key sentences I have recently practiced include:

  • "I unfortunately have to work." – Jeg må dessverre jobbe.
  • "Jeg drar ofte på telttur i skogen." – I often go camping in the forest.
  • "Elsa og Mari, spiller dere tennis?" – Elsa and Mari, do you play tennis?
  • "Han har allerede en hund." – He already has a dog.
  • "Jeg har ikke lyst, og jeg må jobbe." – I don't want to, and I have to work.
  • "Drar du til matbutikken i dag?" – Are you going to the grocery store today?
  • "Hvem er guttene?" – Who are the boys?
  • "My daughter is sixteen years old." – Datteren min er seksten år.
  • "Hva studerer studentene?" – What are the students studying?
  • "Hvem er mennene?" – Who are the men?
  • "How do you say 'parent'?" – forelder
  • "Om hundre år" – In a hundred years
  • "Your appointment is at two o'clock." – Timen din er klokka to.
  • "Do you have a headache?" – Har du vondt i hodet?
  • "What does the round-trip ticket cost?" – Hva koster tur-retur-billetten?
  • "Vil du avbestille billetten din?" – Would you like to cancel your ticket?
  • "My roommate doesn't have a brother." – Romkameraten min har ikke en bror.
  • "I don't have a headache anymore." – Jeg har ikke vondt i hodet lenger.
  • "Sønnen min har ikke feber lenger." – My son doesn't have a fever anymore.
  • "The cat is sleeping on the couch." – Katten sover på sofaen.
  • "They're ordering tea at the cafe." – De bestiller te på kafeen.
  • "Er du ikke tørst lenger?" – Aren't you thirsty anymore?
  • "I want to go to the movie theater." – Jeg vil gå til kinoen.
  • "No, but I can come tonight." – Nei, men jeg kan komme i kveld.
  • "When does bus number fourteen come?" – Når kommer buss nummer fjorten?
  • "When does bus number thirteen come?" – Når kommer buss nummer tretten?

Each mistake is an opportunity. I am using these reviews to solidify my understanding of word order, prepositions, and common conversational phrases.

Conversations I Can Now Have

🏥 Health & Symptoms:
"Jeg har vondt i hodet og feber." – I have a headache and a fever.
"Er du fortsatt syk?" – Are you still sick?
"Jeg er ikke trött lenger." – I'm not tired anymore.
"Sønnen min har ikke feber lenger." – My son doesn't have a fever anymore.
🎉 Planning Celebrations:
"Vi skal feire bursdagen min i juli." – We are going to celebrate my birthday in July.
"Jeg sender invitasjoner til gjestene." – I am sending invitations to the guests.
"Det er ballonger og en klovn på festen." – There are balloons and a clown at the party.
"Hurra! Gratulerer med dagen!" – Hooray! Happy birthday!
🚍 Giving Directions:
"Gå rett frem omtrent tjue meter." – Go straight ahead about twenty meters.

"Når kommer buss nummer fjorten?" – When does bus number fourteen come?
🍽️ Dining Out:

Grammar Insights

  • Present tense verbs: danser, går, tar, bestiller, sove, jobber, studerer
  • Ordinal numbers: første (first), fjerde (fourth), femte (fifth), sjette (sixth)
  • Prepositional phrases: i morgen (tomorrow), i juli (in July), hjemme (at home), i sengen (in bed), i skogen (in the forest)
  • Adjective usage: rød ballong (red balloon), søt dessert (sweet dessert), trist (sad), tørst (thirsty), vennlig (friendly)
  • Negation patterns: "ikke ... lenger" (not ... anymore) – e.g., "Jeg har ikke vondt i hodet lenger."
  • Question formation: "Er du ...?" "Vil du ...?" "Når kommer ...?" "Hvem er ...?" "Hva studerer ...?"
  • Modal verbs: må (must), kan (can), vil (want)

Reflections on 307 Days

Reaching 307 days with over 129,000 XP and multiple Diamond League wins feels surreal. When I started, I could not have imagined this level of consistency. But what is even more meaningful is how the language has started to feel like mine, not just a set of flashcards, but a living system of expression.

The shift toward speaking practice has been a game-changer. Reading and listening are passive skills; speaking forces me to produce, to make mistakes in real time, and to find my voice in Norwegian. I am using shadowing (repeating after audio), reading aloud, and even talking to myself in Norwegian during daily routines.

I have also noticed that reviewing mistakes, especially the ones I have made repeatedly, has dramatically improved my accuracy. The mistake-review sessions (with +20 XP each) have become a daily ritual.
